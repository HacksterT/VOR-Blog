Most of us remember the moment we turned to God. The altar. The prayer. The tears. The relief.

And then Monday came.

The week after I got saved, I woke up with the same temptations I had the week before. The same anger. The same appetite for approval. The same instinct to run my own life. Nothing had changed except that now I knew it was wrong, and that made it worse. I felt like a fraud. Wasn't I supposed to be new?

2 Corinthians 5:17 says "if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away; behold, the new has come." I read that and thought I was broken. If the old was supposed to pass away, why was it still showing up for breakfast?

It took me years to realize I was reading it wrong. Not the verse. My timeline.

### The new creation is positional, not instantaneous

Paul wrote that to a church fighting division, sexual immorality, lawsuits among believers, and getting drunk at communion. Read 1 Corinthians and tell me those people had it all figured out. They did not. But they were new creations anyway — not because the old nature had evaporated, but because their position before God had changed. They belonged to Him now. The direction of their life had reversed.

That is the part I missed. Repentance is not a moment you pass through. It is a direction you walk in.

The Greek word is *metanoia*. It means a change of mind — a turning. Not a one-time pivot. A continuous reorientation. Every morning you wake up and the old nature is still there, making its case. The flesh does not retire. It does not get reformed. Paul says in Romans 7:18 that nothing good dwells in it. That does not stop being true at conversion. It is still true at breakfast.

The difference is that before Christ, the flesh was the only voice in the room. After Christ, there is a new voice. The Spirit. And every day you get to decide which one you follow.

### The daily act of surrender

Romans 12:1-2 calls us to "present your bodies as a living sacrifice" and to "be transformed by the renewal of your mind." Notice the tense. Present. Be transformed. Ongoing. Continuous. Not "presented once" or "were transformed."

A living sacrifice is the hardest kind, because it keeps crawling off the altar.

I get up in the morning and my first instinct is to check my phone — not my Bible. I sit down to work and my first instinct is to protect my reputation — not to serve. I get into a disagreement and my first instinct is to win — not to listen. Every single one of those moments is a fork in the road. The flesh pulls one way. The Spirit pulls the other.

Repentance is not the big dramatic confession after you have already wrecked something. It is the hundred small turns you make throughout the day, choosing the Spirit's direction over the flesh's default.

This is why the disciplines matter. Prayer is not a ritual. It is turning your attention from yourself to God. Scripture is not a checkbox. It is turning your mind from the noise of the world to the voice of the Creator. Confession is not groveling. It is turning your secrets into the light so they lose their power over you.

None of these earn you anything. They do not make God love you more. But they quiet the flesh just enough that you can hear the Spirit, and that is often the difference between turning and drifting.

### You will fall asleep

Jesus told the parable of the ten virgins. All ten fell asleep waiting for the bridegroom. Not just the foolish ones — all of them. The wise ones were ready when he came because they had prepared beforehand. The foolish ones had not.

You will drift. You will get distracted. You will have days — maybe weeks — where you are running on empty and you do not even notice until the flame is almost out. That is not evidence that you were never saved. It is evidence that you are human.

The question is not whether you will drift. The question is whether you have built the habits that bring you back. When the call comes — and it comes every morning, not just at the end of the age — is there oil in your lamp? Or are you running to the store?

### The daily 180

You do not need to manufacture a feeling. You do not need to climb back to some spiritual high you remember from years ago. You just need to turn.

Right now, wherever you are, turn your attention toward God. Not toward your failures. Not toward your to-do list. Not toward the person you are mad at or the fear that is eating at you. Toward Him.

That is repentance. Not the guilt. Not the self-loathing. Not the promise to do better. The turn itself.

Acts 3:19 puts it plainly: "Repent therefore, and turn back, that your sins may be blotted out, that times of refreshing may come from the presence of the Lord."

The refreshing comes after the turn. Not before. You do not wait until you feel holy. You turn, and the refreshing follows.

Maybe you turned to God years ago and you have been drifting ever since. Maybe you turned last week and already feel like you are losing altitude. Maybe you have never turned at all and you are just now realizing that the life you have been building is running on fumes.

None of that disqualifies you from turning today. The direction is always open. The Spirit is not standing at the end of a long road with His arms crossed, waiting for you to clean up first. He is right here. Turn.

---

*Father, I turn to You. Not because I have it together, but because I do not. The old nature is still here, still loud, still making its case. I want the new one. I want the Spirit's voice to be the one I follow today. Renew my mind. Quiet my flesh. Remind me that I am not a fraud — I am a work in progress, and You are the one doing the work. In Jesus' name, amen.*
