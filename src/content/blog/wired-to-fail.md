It is eleven at night and you did it again.

I do not need to know what it was. You know. And you know the hour after it, which is the part nobody preaches about — the flat quiet, the arithmetic starting up in your head about how long it had been this time and whether that counts for anything.

Then the promise. Never again. You have made it before. You will probably make it again tomorrow.

I want to tell you something as a physician before I say anything to you as a Christian, because it will save you from a conclusion you are about to draw about yourself.

You did not fail because you are weak. You failed because you were outmatched.

### What is actually happening in your body

I have spent almost thirty years asking people to change their behavior. Stop smoking. Lose forty pounds. Take the medication. Put down the bottle. I have delivered the same clear, correct, urgent instructions thousands of times to people who wanted to obey them.

Most of them did not. Not because they did not care — I have watched people weep in the exam room about it — but because of how the machine is built.

Your reward system does not run on pleasure. It runs on *anticipation*. The dopamine surge that drives you is largest before you get the thing, not while you have it. That is why the reaching is so much stronger than the satisfying, and why the satisfying never quite settles the account. You are not chasing the thing. You are chasing the moment before the thing.

And the system adapts. Repeat anything rewarding and the response flattens, so the same behavior returns less and demands more. That is not a character defect. It is the same physiology that lets your eyes adjust to a dark room, running in a direction that ruins you.

Then there is the cue. The reward loop attaches itself to whatever was nearby — a time of day, a room, a mood, a screen, a particular kind of loneliness. Months later you can walk into that room having thought about none of it, and the craving arrives *before you do*. It is faster than your deliberation because it is not using your deliberation.

And the part of you that is supposed to override all of this — the executive, the adult in the room — is metabolically expensive and easily degraded. Lose sleep, run a stressful week, skip meals, sit in grief, and it browns out. Which is precisely when the loop comes for you.

This is why most people who lose significant weight regain it. Why relapse is the expected course in addiction rather than the exception. Why "just decide to stop" has one of the worst track records in all of medicine.

You are not failing at something everybody else finds easy. You are losing a fight you were structurally set up to lose.

### Now the part that is not an excuse

I need you to hear the next sentence as clearly as the last ones.

None of that gets you off the hook. It explains you. It does not absolve you.

I have handed people that explanation in an exam room and watched relief cross their face, and I have learned to be careful with it — because there is a version of "I am wired this way" that becomes a permanent address. The man who accepts the diagnosis and then uses it as a roof over his head has not been helped. He has been sedated.

The Bible had this described long before anyone imaged a striatum. *"For I do not do the good I want, but the evil I do not want is what I keep on doing"* (Romans 7:19). That is not a poetic flourish. That is a man reporting a split between his intention and his behavior and finding it in himself, not in his circumstances. And Paul does not conclude that he is therefore not responsible. He concludes that he needs rescuing.

Both things are true at once, and you have to hold them together or you will end up in one of two ditches. You are genuinely overmatched, *and* the turn is genuinely yours to make.

I wrote a whole book on this because I do not think most Christians believe it: the flesh is not a metaphor. When Paul sets the works of the flesh against the fruit of the Spirit in Galatians 5, he is not being figurative about the first half and literal about the second. There is a real appetite in real tissue that wants what it wants, and it does not negotiate, and it does not retire at conversion.

### Why the church's version of the treatment plan also fails

Here is where it turns from medicine to something worse.

When a treatment plan fails four times, a good clinician stops blaming the patient and asks whether the plan is wrong. The church, faced with the same failure, usually does the opposite. It prescribes more of it.

More discipline. Tighter fences. A stricter rule, an earlier alarm, another accountability partner, another vow at another altar. And when that fails — because it fails, for the reasons above — the lesson you draw is not that the plan was wrong. The lesson you draw is that you are worse than you thought.

So you tighten it again. That is a man taking a larger dose of a drug that was never indicated.

Paul asks the Galatians a question I cannot read without wincing, because I have deserved it: *"Having begun by the Spirit, are you now being perfected by the flesh?"* (Galatians 3:3).

Read that against everything I just told you about the machine. He is asking whether you have taken a problem that only God can solve and quietly handed it back to the exact system that is generating the problem. You are trying to out-discipline your appetite using a faculty your appetite routinely defeats.

It will not work. It has never worked. It is not working now, which is why you are reading this at eleven at night.

### What actually changes

Not a rule. Rules are external. Your problem is internal and it is upstream of your rules.

What has to change is what you *want* — and you cannot want differently by deciding to. That is the trap, and everyone who has tried to white-knuckle a desire into submission knows it from the inside.

Which is exactly what God said He would do about it: *"I will give you a new heart, and a new spirit I will put within you. I will remove the heart of stone from your flesh and give you a heart of flesh"* (Ezekiel 36:26).

Not new instructions. New tissue. He is not proposing to make you better at obeying. He is proposing to replace the organ that does not want to.

And grace is not the permission slip people accuse it of being. Grace is the power to live as you were meant to. Anybody who tells you grace means the standard has moved is selling you something that will not hold. The standard has not moved an inch. What changed is who is doing the work.

### Self-hatred is not humility

One more thing, because this is where good people get lost.

When you fail and you turn on yourself — three days of low-grade self-loathing, denying yourself things nobody asked you to give up, running yourself down in your own head — you experience that as taking sin seriously.

It is not humility. It is a payment plan.

You are trying to settle the debt in the only currency you have, which is your own misery. You would not say it out loud, but underneath is a calculation: if I suffer enough over this, it will be squared away.

That ledger does not balance. It never has. The bill was paid in full by someone who owed nothing, *while the ink was still wet* — *"God shows his love for us in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us"* (Romans 5:8). Adding your own suffering to a settled account is not reverence. It is an insult to the payment, wearing reverence as a costume.

### Where you actually are

Three things, and collapsing them into one is what keeps people in torment.

There is a **door** — justification. A change of legal standing that happened to you and is finished. You did not build it and you cannot un-build it.

There is a **hallway** — sanctification. The long work of becoming like Christ, which takes the rest of your life. Some weeks you move down it well. Some weeks you shuffle. Some weeks you lose ground.

There is a **room at the end** — glorification. The day it is finally done, and not by you: *"he who began a good work in you will bring it to completion at the day of Jesus Christ"* (Philippians 1:6).

Hold onto this at eleven at night: **a step backward in the hallway is still a step taken in the hallway.** The door is behind you. Losing ground is not being put back outside. Convincing you otherwise is the whole strategy, because a man who believes he has been thrown out stops walking.

### The decision

Now I am going to stop explaining and ask you something, and I am not going to dress it up.

Everything above is true and none of it happens to you automatically.

You have to turn. Not admire the idea of turning. Not agree with this essay. Not feel moved for twenty minutes and go to bed. *Turn* — put down the ledger, stop running your life on your own authority, and hand it to the one who paid for it. *"If you confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved"* (Romans 10:9).

Nobody can do this on your behalf. Not your pastor, not your spouse, not the version of you that keeps promising to start Monday. Growing up in church does not do it. Respecting Jesus does not do it. Reading this does not do it.

And it is not a one-time transaction you completed years ago and can point back to like a receipt. It is tonight, and tomorrow morning, and the morning after that. Repentance is direction, not an anniversary.

So do it now, in the chair you are in, in whatever words you actually have. Not a formula. Say the true thing: *I did it again. I cannot fix this. I am not going to try to pay for it. I am yours.*

You will need to say it again. That is not failure. That is the hallway.

The sin knows your address. It has been here before and it is coming back. But it does not hold the deed, and it never did.
